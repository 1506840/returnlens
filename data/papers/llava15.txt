Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning
Haotian Liu, Chunyuan Li, Yuheng Li, Yong Jae Lee
University of Wisconsin-Madison / Microsoft Research, Redmond
arXiv:2310.03744v2 (CVPR 2024 highlight)
Local extract for ResearchLens seed corpus.

Abstract
Large multimodal models (LMM) have recently shown encouraging progress with visual instruction tuning. In this paper, we present the first systematic study to investigate the design choices of LMMs in a controlled setting under the LLaVA framework. We show that the fully-connected vision-language connector in LLaVA is surprisingly powerful and data-efficient. With simple modifications to LLaVA, namely, using CLIP-ViT-L-336px with an MLP projection and adding academic-task-oriented VQA data with response formatting prompts, we establish stronger baselines that achieve state-of-the-art across 11 benchmarks. Our final 13B checkpoint uses merely 1.2M publicly available data, and finishes full training in ~1 day on a single 8-A100 node. Furthermore, we present some early exploration of open problems in LMMs, including scaling to higher resolution inputs, compositional capabilities, and model hallucination, etc. We hope this makes state-of-the-art LMM research more accessible.

1 Introduction (key claims)
LLaVA excels in conversational-style visual reasoning and outperforms later approaches like InstructBLIP on such benchmarks, while InstructBLIP excels in traditional VQA benchmarks demanding single-word or short answers. This work originates from LLaVA and builds a road map from input, model, and data perspectives:
(1) Scaling to high-resolution image inputs by dividing images into grids; increased resolution improves detailed perception and reduces hallucination.
(2) Compositional capabilities: training on long-form language reasoning together with shorter visual reasoning can improve writing capability for multimodal questions.
(3) Data efficiency: randomly downsampling the training mixture by up to 75% does not significantly decrease performance.
(4) Data scaling: scaling of data granularity in conjunction with model capability is crucial for improved capability without introducing artifacts like hallucination.

3.2 Response Format Prompting
A single response formatting prompt ("Answer the question using a single word or phrase.") appended to VQA questions lets LLaVA adjust output format per instruction, avoiding ChatGPT post-processing of answers. Merely including VQAv2 in training lifts MME from 809.6 to 1323.8, outperforming InstructBLIP by 111 points.

3.3 Scaling the Data and Model
Two-layer MLP vision-language connector improves multimodal capability over the original linear projection. Adding academic-task-oriented VQA (OKVQA, A-OKVQA, OCRVQA, TextCaps) plus region-level VQA (Visual Genome, RefCOCO) improves localization of fine-grained visual details. Input resolution scaled to 336^2 via CLIP-ViT-L-336px; LLM scaled to 13B; ShareGPT added. Final model = LLaVA-1.5. Training cost ~2x LLaVA: ~6h pretraining + ~20h instruction tuning on 8xA100.

3.4 Scaling to Higher Resolutions (LLaVA-1.5-HD)
Existing open-source CLIP encoders cap at 336^2. Instead of positional embedding interpolation (which needs large-scale finetuning and fixes inference resolution), the image is divided into smaller patches at the encoder's native resolution, encoded independently, merged into one large feature map, plus a downsampled global-context feature concatenated. Supports arbitrary resolution while keeping data efficiency. Global context ablation: GQA 62.9 -> 63.8, MME 1425.8 -> 1497.5, MM-Vet 31.9 -> 35.1.

4 Empirical Evaluation
Evaluated on 12 benchmarks total: VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA-IMG, TextVQA (academic); POPE, MME-Perception, MMBench (en/cn), SEED-Bench, LLaVA-Bench-in-the-Wild, MM-Vet (instruction-following). LLaVA-1.5 achieves best overall performance despite magnitudes smaller pretraining/instruction-tuning data. Key numbers: LLaVA-1.5-13B VQAv2 80.0, GQA 63.3, TextVQA 61.3, POPE adv 84.5, MME 1531.3, MMBench-en 67.7, MM-Vet 36.1. LLaVA-1.5-13B-HD further improves detail-heavy tasks (OCR in MM-Vet, detailed description).

Emerging properties: generalizes to unseen response format instructions (VizWiz unanswerable 11.1% -> 67.8%); multilingual visual conversation emerges without any multilingual visual instruction finetuning, attributed to ShareGPT; LLaVA-1.5 outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat on MMBench-CN by +7.3% despite no Chinese multimodal tuning.

5 Open Problems in LMMs
5.1 Data efficiency: with only 50% of samples the model keeps >98% of full-dataset performance; MMBench/ScienceQA/POPE do not decrease at all, suggesting less-is-more potential.
5.2 Rethinking hallucination: hallucination is significantly reduced when scaling inputs to 448^2. When input resolution is insufficient to discern details present in training data at that granularity, and the amount of such data is large enough, the model *learns* to hallucinate. There must be a balance between richer annotation and model capability at that granularity.
5.3 Compositional capabilities: correctly answering an object attribute in VQA does not guarantee accurate depiction of that attribute in a whole-image detailed description; some languages (e.g. Korean) still fall behind. Deeper understanding of the mechanism could further improve capability and data efficiency.

6 Conclusion
Proposes a simple, effective, data-efficient baseline (LLaVA-1.5) and explores open problems: higher-resolution scaling, hallucination, compositional capabilities. Hopes these reproducible baselines provide a reference for future research in open-source LMM.

=== Limitations (main text, verbatim) ===
Limitations. Despite the promising results demonstrated by LLaVA-1.5, it still has limitations including prolonged training for high-resolution images, lack of multiple-image understanding, limited problem solving capabilities in certain fields. It is not exempt from producing hallucinations, and should be used with caution in critical applications (e.g. medical). See appendix for a detailed discussion.

=== Appendix C Limitations (verbatim) ===
Despite the promising results demonstrated by LLaVA-1.5, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, LLaVA-1.5 utilizes full image patches, potentially prolonging each training iteration. While visual resamplers reduce the number of visual patches in LLMs, they currently cannot achieve convergence as efficiently as LLaVA with a comparable amount of training data, probably due to more trainable parameters in the resamplers. The development of a sample-efficient visual resampler could pave the way for future scaling-up of instruction-following multimodal models. Second, LLaVA-1.5 is not yet capable of processing multiple images due to the lack of such instruction-following data, and the limit of the context length. Third, although LLaVA-1.5 exhibits proficiency in following complex instructions, its problem-solving capabilities can still be limited in certain domains, which could be improved with a more capable language model and with high-quality, targeted visual instruction tuning data. Finally, despite its significantly reduced propensity for hallucination, LLaVA-1.5 is not exempt from producing hallucinations and occasionally disseminating misinformation, and should be used with caution in critical applications (e.g. medical).
