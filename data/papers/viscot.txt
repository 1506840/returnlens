Visual CoT: Advancing Multi-Modal Language Models with a Comprehensive Dataset and Benchmark for Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
Hao Shao, Shengju Qian, Han Xiao, Guanglu Song, Zhuofan Zong, Letian Wang, Yu Liu, Hongsheng Li
CUHK / SenseTime Research / U Toronto / Shanghai AI Lab — arXiv:2403.16999v3 (NeurIPS 2024 D&B spotlight)
Local extract for ResearchLens seed corpus.

Abstract
Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated impressive performance in various VQA tasks. However, they often lack interpretability and struggle with complex visual inputs, especially when the resolution of the input image is high or when the interested region that could provide key information for answering the question is small. We collect and introduce the large-scale Visual CoT dataset comprising 438k question-answer pairs, annotated with intermediate bounding boxes highlighting key regions essential for answering the questions. Additionally, about 98k pairs of them are annotated with detailed reasoning steps. We propose a multi-turn processing pipeline that dynamically focuses on visual inputs and provides interpretable thoughts. We also introduce the related benchmark to evaluate the MLLMs in scenarios requiring specific local region identification.

1 Introduction (key claims)
Most MLLMs and related benchmarks are trained to respond to instructions based on visual inputs using a decoder-only autoregressive design as a single black box; they suffer from inaccurate information and even hallucinations, and the black-box design hinders interpretability. The potential of multi-turn in-context capability and the advantage of chain-of-thought for LLMs have not been extensively explored in MLLMs. Prior works (multimodal-CoT, MM-REACT-style) improve via text-level CoT or in-context learning, but it remains uncharted whether MLLMs can benefit from CoT inside the *visual understanding* process.
Humans scan the whole image, locate references, then focus on targets; most MLLMs process aligned image context at a fixed grain with large computation (CLIP / EVA2-CLIP / InternVL), so they cannot dynamically zoom into adjusted context and instead seek information primarily from the text domain.
Two obstacles to building such pipelines: (a) existing VQA datasets lack intermediate visual CoT supervision; (b) popular MLLM pipelines rely on static image context inputs.
Contributions: 438k visual-CoT dataset across five domains with per-sample bbox (+~98k with detailed reasoning steps); a novel multi-turn pipeline that dynamically focuses visual inputs and emits interpretable thoughts; a visual-CoT benchmark for local-region-dependent evaluation.

3 Visual CoT Dataset
Domains deliberately chosen: Text/doc (OCR + contextual understanding), Fine-grained understanding (subtle visual differences), Charts (graphical data for business/science), General VQA (breadth), Relation Reasoning (spatial/contextual awareness). Sources include TextVQA, TextCaps, DocVQA, DUDE, SROIE, Birds-200-2011, Flickr30k, Visual7W, InfographicsVQA, VSR, GQA, Open Images. Linguistic annotation by GPT-4; visual annotation by PaddleOCR; a filtering pipeline improves content quality.
Dataset analysis (§3.2): average CoT bbox size is 247.82 px, matching common vision-encoder input resolutions of 224-336 px, while original images are usually far larger and must be downsampled, losing information. Key regions occupy only ~13.2% of image area. If the model fails to identify these regions, most of the processed image is irrelevant -> inefficient computation, hallucination, degraded performance.

4 Enhancing MLLMs with CoT capabilities (VisCoT framework)
CoT prompt: "Please provide the bounding box coordinate of the region that can help you answer the question better." The model predicts a bbox; a visual sampler crops the localized image X1; the same vision encoder + projector yields H1; the MLLM integrates {H0, H1} from original and localized images to answer. During training the GT bbox is used instead of the prediction. Data without CoT annotations skips the crop branch, so the baseline handles annotated and non-annotated data simultaneously.
Visual sampler: center + half-width/half-height computed; sample size s = max(max(w_half, h_half), res_half) to satisfy CLIP's square receptive field; if the crop exceeds image bounds the center is shifted inward, which mitigates detection inaccuracies.
Training: two stages as in LLaVA-1.5 (freeze vision encoder + LLM for alignment; all weights trainable in stage 2). Model uses CLIP ViT-L/14 + Vicuna-7/13B, linear projection like LLaVA.

5 Experiments
Benchmark: 12 source datasets; official splits where available; SROIE/DUDE/Visual7W test splits used for zero-shot visual-CoT evaluation; scoring by ChatGPT assigning a 0-1 numerical score.
Main results on the Visual CoT benchmark (Table 3, average of 12 datasets): LLaVA-1.5-7B 0.454, LLaVA-1.5-13B 0.478, SPHINX-13B 0.419, VisCoT-7B@224^2 0.550, VisCoT-7B@336^2 0.580.
Per-dataset @336^2 vs LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline: DocVQA .476 vs .244; TextCaps .675 vs .597; TextVQA .775 vs .588; DUDE .386 vs .290; SROIE .470 vs .136; Flickr30k .668 vs .581; Visual7W .558 vs .575; GQA .631 vs .534; Open Images .822 vs .412; VSR .614 vs .572; Birds-200-2011 .559 vs .530.
Chart is the one regressing domain: InfographicsVQA .287 at 224^2 and .324 at 336^2, both below LLaVA-1.5-7B's .400 -- i.e. visual-CoT supervision does not transfer to chart reasoning without in-domain training splits.
Ablation — bbox selection strategy (average): w/o CoT 0.443; Random 0.453; Center 0.490; Baseline (predicted bbox) 0.550; GT BBox 0.752. Random box selection performs similarly to no-CoT, i.e. an incorrect prediction buys nothing; center helps because central regions are often informative. The GT-vs-predicted gap (0.752 vs 0.550) is the headroom ceiling.
Ablation — token efficiency: with visual CoT, accuracy at 224 resolution surpasses the standard pipeline at 448 while using half the visual tokens.
Ablation — visual sampler: expanded cropping and centered cropping each help; both together give 0.496 -> 0.550 average.
Appendix C: CoT bbox detection Top-1 Acc@0.5 is low on document sets (DocVQA 20.4, DUDE 9.6, InfographicsVQA 10.0 at 336^2) though strong on natural-image relation sets (Flickr30k 51.3, GQA 49.5, VSR/Birds 47-67).
Appendix D: without invoking CoT, VisCoT remains competitive on 8 standard MLLM benchmarks and tops REC (RefCOCOg val-u 88.34 at 336^2), beating specialist models G-DINO-L and UNINEXT.

=== Appendix F Limitations (verbatim) ===
In scenarios where the input image contains extensive information or the question is particularly complex, VisCoT may struggle to identify the most relevant region for answering the question. As shown in Figure 7, this challenge can sometimes result in the model being misled and producing incorrect responses.
Our data pipeline inherits the limitations of utilizing GPT-4 API. (1) Accuracy and Misinformation: Generated content may not always be accurate, which could lead to the spread of misinformation. To mitigate this, we have designed a comprehensive filtering script as a post-process to improve content quality. (2) Bias and Fairness: Since we do not have access to the training data of GPT-4, the generated instructional data might reflect inherent biases, potentially reinforcing social or cultural inequalities present in the base model training.
